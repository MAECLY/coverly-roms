Every ROM gets its cover.

Coverly ROMs checks your handheld's SD card, shows which games have no cover art and adds it in one go. It rehearses first, and nothing you already have is touched unless you say so.

Four steps. The first two write nothing.

  1. 1

    Open the folder or SD card.

    Coverly recognises your firmware's or frontend's layout and shows every system with what it lacks: cover, screenshot or title screen. Libraries of more than 10,000 games too.

  2. 2

    Rehearse the job first.

    Each ROM is identified by its fingerprint (CRC32, MD5 or SHA1), or failing that by its name. The wizard picks the image by the ROM's region, your sources or the votes, and shows you the whole plan.

    Nothing is written until you press Run.

  3. 3

    Decide only the doubtful ones.

    Confident matches go through on their own. Doubtful ones wait in a queue you can clear from the keyboard, and the pick tells you why it was picked.

  4. 4

    Apply it, and undo it if you change your mind.

    Images are written one at a time, never left half-written. Undo puts the card back the way it was. And any cover can be swapped by hand from the game's panel.

Your card comes back as it was, plus covers.

Dry run by default
Writing is something you ask for. On the command line, with --apply.
Only what is missing
Covers you already have stay unless you choose to replace them, and then a copy is kept first.
Backups off the card
Copies and each job's log go to the app's data folder, not to the SD card.
Nothing outside the library
It never writes outside the folder you opened, and file names stay valid on FAT and exFAT.
One failure stops nothing
If one ROM fails, the rest carry on. Offline or rate-limited, the job pauses and you resume it later.

Same engine, in the terminal: coverly
# What is missing (read-only)
$ coverly audit /Volumes/SD --missing-only
4553 missing

# The plan, nothing written
$ coverly fill /Volumes/SD
plan: 4553 · 0 written

# For real, skipping the doubtful ones
$ coverly fill /Volumes/SD --apply --auto-only
✓ 4401 applied

# Changed your mind
$ coverly undo
↶ 4401 restored

Opening it the first time

  • macOS: if it says the developer cannot be verified, go to System Settings, Privacy & Security, and click “Open Anyway”.
  • Windows: if SmartScreen steps in, click “More info”, then “Run anyway”.
  • Linux: make the AppImage executable, or install the .deb.

They warn because the downloads carry no Apple or Microsoft certificate. Check each file against SHA256SUMS.

What it does not do yet

  • ScreenScraper: planned, not available yet.
  • Videos, mixed images, marquees or wheels.
  • Editing metadata, renaming or verifying ROMs.
  • Running on the handheld itself.
  • It has not been tested on real handhelds yet.

Before you ask.

Is it free?

Yes. It costs nothing, needs no account and has no paid tier. The code is AGPL-3.0.

Does it come with ROMs or cover art?

No. Coverly ROMs bundles no ROMs, BIOS files or game art. Images are downloaded from libretro-thumbnails and, if you add your own key, SteamGridDB. Every image belongs to its owners.

Will it overwrite my covers?

Not unless you ask it to. By default it only fills what is missing. If you choose to replace art, it first keeps a copy in the app's data folder, not on the card, and Undo puts it back.

Do I need an account?

No. libretro-thumbnails works without one. SteamGridDB is optional and uses your own key, kept in your system keychain.

Which handhelds does it work with?

Any that uses the Anbernic stock, GarlicOS, Onion OS, MinUI and NextUI, muOS, ES-DE, Batocera, Knulli and EmulationStation, or RetroArch layout. For other firmware you can build your own layout with a live preview. It has not been tested on real handhelds yet.

Why do Gatekeeper and SmartScreen warn me?

Because the downloads carry no Apple or Microsoft certificate. The first time, you just confirm that you want to open it, and every release comes with a SHA256SUMS file to check its downloads against.

Does it send data anywhere?

There is no telemetry. The app only talks to the art sources and, unless you turn it off, to the update check at startup. Images are re-encoded, so they carry no metadata.

How does it update?

From inside the app, with GitHub Releases builds verified with minisign before they install. There are stable and beta channels, and it never restarts in the middle of a job. The command line uses coverly update.
